Concept

Less is more, in Castile

MIES. 1. (Spanish) Ripe cereal ready to be harvested, the base of the Castilian landscape. 2. (Arch.) Architecture built on material and structural clarity.

Modular furniture built on a conceptual inversion: the fragility of cereal stalks becomes aluminium beams. Its trapezoidal geometry allows spatial groupings based on the pentagon.

Construction system

Aluminium beams and poplar shelves

The poplar plywood shelves sit in fixed positions set by precise cuts that, once slotted into the beams, define the structure. Holes in the profiles take tensioned cords and hold A1 panels.

Poplar links the piece to the forest resources of Castilla y León; aluminium brings industrial precision.

Configurations

From circle to zigzag

In a circle, the modules create open meeting areas; alternating in a zigzag, the structure stretches along walkways. Open configurations work as small shelters that gently divide the space.

Assembly

From the bottom up

  1. The base is set down to stabilise the assembly.
  2. The vertical beams that form the structural skeleton are slotted in.
  3. With the frame up, the shelves go in from the bottom up.
Packaging

Recycled raffia bag

High-strength recycled agricultural raffia. The handles let two people carry the piece, so installing it is shared work from the start.
